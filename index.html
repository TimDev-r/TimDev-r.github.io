03 Try the work yourself

Two real problems from my computer-vision work, running live in your browser. No background needed — each one explains itself as you play with it.

Finding the few seconds that matter in hours of footage

motion signatures motion-groundtruth-tools

The problem: a ten-hour recording is almost entirely nothing happening, and someone has to find the moments that aren't.

The idea: squeeze every frame down to a single line of text showing how far away anything moved. Hours of video become a page you can skim. Below, the strip is that line — left is close to the camera, right is far away. Tap or drag the person to see it react.

3.0 m
0 mclose2 m4 m6 m8 mfar

  • - nothing moved at this distance
  • x slight movement
  • + someone moving

Notice two marks appear, not one. Moving reveals the wall behind you, and the camera counts that as movement too — so a single person shows up twice: once where they are, once where they were. Knowing to expect that is the difference between trusting the data and chasing a phantom bug.

The technical version

Depth is sampled from the current frame, so pixels the subject has just vacated report the background distance rather than the subject's. The signature therefore carries a disocclusion trail — inherent to differencing against a single depth map, not a defect. It's pinned down by a test in the repository so it can't silently change.

Counting people without counting anyone twice

cross-camera matching realsense-multicam-detection

The problem: two cameras watch the same room. One person walks in, and both cameras see them. Is that one person, or two?

The idea: don't compare what the cameras see — compare where they think the person is standing. Two sightings of the same spot in the room must be the same person. Tap or drag to move the person around.

Slide the cameras apart and watch it break. Each camera measures from itself, so the further apart they sit, the further apart their answers look — and the system decides one person is two. Tick Line the cameras up first and it works again at any distance.

That's a genuine open bug in my own code, and I've left it in rather than quietly fixing the demo: it's documented in the repository, and this is what it looks like.

The technical version

depth_matching.py compares each camera's own camera-space points without transforming them through the stored extrinsics, so the separation can never fall below the baseline and the 10 cm match threshold effectively just tests the baseline. The calibration step writes valid R and T to calibration_data.npz — they are simply never applied. Checking the box applies them, collapsing the gap to sensor noise.
